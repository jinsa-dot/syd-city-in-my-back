City in My Back

About

Some cities stay in front of you. Sydney is the one I carry behind me. I keep walking forward, and the harbour, the heat and the late ferries keep following.

You never see the city behind you all at once. You turn your head and catch one picture, squeezed or stretched by how far you turned. Each frame you draw is one photograph. Draw enough of them and the whole city is there, at your back.

How to look

  • Move the cursor and you leave footprints. You keep walking; they stay behind you and fade. Click to tear the picture.
  • Draw a frame. A whole photograph appears inside it, stretched or squeezed to fit.
  • Every frame shows a different photograph, until you have seen all of them.
  • A photograph stays clear only while you look back at it. Move away and its colours bleach out within a few seconds. It stays in black and white for about fifteen seconds, then slowly disappears, and once it has gone it never comes back.
  • If you leave the page while a photograph is still there, it waits for you, bleached and older. Look back at it quickly or it will go.
  • Draw a frame across almost the whole screen to turn around.
  • Small sentences drift across the screen, moved by today's Sydney weather. Hover over one to stop it and read it.
  • Sound: every click is a footstep walking away, every photograph a shutter.
  • Click the temperature to switch between °C and °F.

Weather

Weather is more than a number: it changes what you do today. So each kind of Sydney weather, light, medium or heavy, gets its own small suggestion, and the letters of that sentence move the way the weather moves. Reading it, you feel the weather.
